08Inflation: implications for our decision

A single marginal increase is a risk-management move—not the start of a new hiking cycle.

Switch between core PCE and core CPI. The dashed line is the monthly pace that adds up to 2% a year.

Core PCE, Sep 2026, m/m 0.3% 2.9% 3-month annualized 3.1% year over year
Measure

Evidence supporting a 25 bp hike

  • Inflation is still above target.
  • Core PCE rose 0.3% m/m in August and September; core CPI rose 0.3% for three straight months.
  • Domestic demand remains resilient.
  • Two FOMC members already preferred hiking in October.

Why not hold instead?

  • Policy works with lags.
  • Some inflation is supply-driven and not solved by rates.
  • September real PCE was roughly flat.

Our judgment

  • It keeps pressure on inflation while the economy is still absorbing current rates.

Methods

  • Chart series come from FRED, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, retrieved November 24, 2026.
  • Year over year is the 12-month percent change in a price index; month over month is the 1-month change; 3-month annualized is (index ÷ index three months earlier)4 − 1.
  • A 2% annual rate works out to about 0.165% a month: 1.021/12 − 1.
  • GDP growth and contributions are seasonally adjusted annual rates from BEA; contributions add up to growth, up to rounding.
  • October 2025 household-survey and CPI data were not collected during the federal government shutdown, so those months are blank.
  • On the rate charts, December's step is our proposal (or the pause alternative), placed at the December meeting.
