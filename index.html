October FOMC: starting point

  • Markets anticipated tightening: fed funds futures priced an estimated 88% probability of a 25 bp December hike; the median NY Fed Desk survey respondent expected another hike in March 2027.
  • Credit remained broadly available: solid corporate bond issuance, bank lending standards slightly easier than historical norms, and generally available consumer credit, despite emerging stress among riskier borrowers.

What the moves told us, through Nov 17

  • The 10-year real yield rose 22 bp, suggesting the increase in long-term yields was not driven by rising inflation expectations.
  • Riskier financing is becoming more expensive, but credit remained broadly available.
  • A meaningful repricing in stocks, led by technology, but no established evidence yet that the decline has materially impaired households' present value of lifetime resources (PVLR) or consumption.

10Technical component: a Taylor-rule check

A standard policy rule puts the federal funds rate above today's 3.75%–4.00% range.

Move the assumptions. The prescribed rate, the arithmetic and the history chart all update.

Taylor (1993) rule, today 4.98% +110 bp versus the current 3.875% midpoint 4.04% with gradual adjustment (ρ = 0.85)

Assumptions

4.2%

The arithmetic, today

Intuition

r* and u* cannot be observed, and estimates differ by a point or more. The rule is a benchmark for judgment, not an autopilot.

Rule: Taylor (1993), i = r* + π + 0.5(π − 2) + 0.5 × gap; the balanced-approach rule puts 1.0 on the gap. Output gap from unemployment by Okun's law: gap = 2 × (u* − u). Defaults are the FOMC's September 2026 longer-run medians: fed funds 3.2%, so r* = 3.2 − 2 = 1.2, and unemployment 4.2%. Gradual adjustment: ρ × today's midpoint + (1 − ρ) × rule. Methods

  • Chart series come from FRED, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, retrieved November 24, 2026.
  • Year over year is the 12-month percent change in a price index; month over month is the 1-month change; 3-month annualized is (index ÷ index three months earlier)4 − 1.
  • A 2% annual rate works out to about 0.165% a month: 1.021/12 − 1.
  • GDP growth and contributions are seasonally adjusted annual rates from BEA; contributions add up to growth, up to rounding.
  • October 2025 household-survey and CPI data were not collected during the federal government shutdown, so those months are blank.
  • On the rate charts, December's step is our proposal (or the pause alternative), placed at the December meeting.
  • Markets (07): daily closes through Nov 20 (Nasdaq Nov 19); 10-year breakeven = nominal − real (TIPS) yield; stocks rebased to Oct 28 = 100; ICE BofA option-adjusted spreads shown in basis points.
  • Taylor rule (10): i = r* + π + 0.5(π − 2) + w × 2(u* − u), with w = 0.5 (Taylor 1993) or 1.0 (balanced approach); the factor 2 converts the unemployment gap to an output gap (Okun's law). Defaults use the FOMC's September longer-run medians.
  • Projections (11): FOMC September medians; inflation and GDP are Q4/Q4, unemployment the Q4 average, fed funds the year-end level.
